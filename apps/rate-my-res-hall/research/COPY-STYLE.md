# Res Life copy

The voice is a Richmond student helping someone make a housing list. Be specific and useful, with an occasional joke about the actual situation. Avoid trying to sound like a marketing campaign or imitating the user's spelling and swearing.

## Decisions for this pass

- Describe the action or result: “Which hall houses more people?” replaces “Is yours actually bigger?”
- Don't turn every empty or repeated state into a joke. “Choose a different hall to compare capacities.” replaces “Same hall. Same story.”
- Avoid repeating balanced slogans such as “Same X. Different Y.”, forced contrast (“not just X, but Y”), fake rhetorical questions, and chains of three fragments. These are editorial preferences for this site, not proof that a sentence was machine-written.
- Use everyday words. Remove inflated claims and needless qualifiers. Keep source limitations where they affect a student's decision.
- Keep a joke if it adds something and reads naturally in context. Don't add one just to fill space.
- Vary sentence structure naturally; don't inject typos or randomness to evade detection.

## Evidence

Reinhart et al., *Do LLMs write like humans? Variation in grammatical and rhetorical styles*, PNAS (2025), compares human and model writing across grammatical, lexical and rhetorical features. It reports systematic differences and limited stylistic variation. It does not establish a reliable phrase blacklist or prove authorship from an isolated line.
